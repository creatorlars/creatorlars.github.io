Videomancer

A standalone FPGA video synthesizer and effects console, composite to HDMI.

Problem
One instrument that accepts and outputs every common video format, runs real-time video effects the user can load, and lets an outside developer community write new ones.
Built
  • iCE40 HX4K + RP2040; ADV7611/ADV7513 HDMI in/out; ADV7181C + ADA4412-3 analog input; ADV7393 SD/ED/HD encoder; composite, S-Video, YPbPr and RGB; TLV320AIC3104 audio.
  • RP2040 C++20 firmware on LZX’s shared embedded framework, and an FPGA platform that loads user-selectable real-time video effects (feedback, slit-scan, kaleidoscope…).
  • Effect packages signed with Ed25519 over BLAKE2b-256, and a public GPL-3.0 SDK with GHDL image tests.
Result
The first 240 units shipped in three batches in late 2025; 69 public firmware release candidates; open SDK with outside contributors.

Chromagnon

A 52 HP hybrid instrument: a video-rate analog XY engine under FPGA/MCU control.

Problem
Transform video-rate X/Y scan ramps (rotate, size, skew, curve, fold, key) in analog at full video bandwidth, under digital control and genlock, at a cost a small company can build.
Built
  • Discrete high-speed analog core: MC1496 Gilbert-cell rotators, matched-pair two-quadrant multipliers and VCAs, diode-sigmoid keyer and ±5 V 130 MHz op-amp stages; rightsized from 28 multiplier cells with 56 isolated rails to 8.
  • Replaced the Zynq SoM with iCE40HX4K + RP2040 after costing an all-digital dual-ECP5 alternative. The iCE40 RTL recovers timing from embedded SAV/EAV codes, converts YCbCr to RGB and genlocks 15 SD/ED/HD formats up to 74.25 MHz; the RP2040 writes the transform matrix to DACs.
  • Python instrument model bit-exact to the RTL and GHDL (76/76 injected faults caught in simulation), ported to a live WebGL simulator.
Result
RevJ went from schematic review to a fab-ready 8-layer board in 10 days, then up on the bench: composite and component passthrough, Sync Out and feedback across 15/15 timings, genlock offset 0. On-board silicon cut 64% versus the Zynq revision ($195 to $70), BOM a further 26%.

Embedded C++ firmware

Eight years of instrument firmware, and the shared engine underneath it.

Problem
Every LZX instrument needs firmware that talks to video silicon, runs a real-time UI and modulation, and can be updated in the field, built by a tiny team across several product lines.
Built
  • Firmware for TBC2 and Memory Palace (Zynq-7010, bare-metal and FreeRTOS), Scrolls (STM32F411), and Chromagnon and Videomancer (RP2040).
  • An application engine (state machines, UI, presets, modulation, MIDI, USB) evolved through three generations into a C++20 framework with no exceptions, RTTI, STL containers or heap allocation, shared across three product lines.
  • Drivers for ADV7181C, ADV7393, ADV7611, ADV7513, THS7353, ADA4412-3, MCP4728, ADS1115, TLV320AIC3104 and more, with host-side mock-based unit tests and PC simulators.
Result
One codebase now carries Videomancer, Chromagnon and the next RP2350-class products.

Verification & hardware-in-the-loop

Test systems that judge simulation and real hardware by the same rules.

Problem
An FPGA video instrument with many effects across many video formats can’t be verified by eye, and every release candidate risks re-breaking something already fixed.
Built
  • vmtest: one analysis library (SSIM/PSNR) scores GHDL simulation and real HDMI captures alike; 984-case sweep tier.
  • cgtest: a Python Chromagnon model bit-exact to the RTL, nine block test runners, Rigol MSO5000 automation over SCPI, and 88 golden-vector tests for its WebGL port.
  • ngspice pre-fab reviews with derating rules and IBIS-derived driver models (AI-assisted tooling).
Result
46 named regression guards for Videomancer release-candidate defects; a ~200-check simulation review that caught 9 failures on a clock board before fabrication.
